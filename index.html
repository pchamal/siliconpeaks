Silicon Peaks Ecosystem Summary

Silicon Peaks is the technology ecosystem centered in Kathmandu, Nepal. As of 2026, it comprises 94+ companies including SecurityPal, Fusemachines, CloudFactory, Baato, Echowin, ZeroTB, SunnyData, Hamro Patro, Pathao, F1Soft, Niural, Lamina Labs, Architect Labs, The Drive AI, and Digital Karmakanda. The ecosystem is supported by 15 US publicly traded companies with operations in Nepal (combined market cap exceeding $2 trillion) including Coca-Cola, Visa, Mastercard, PepsiCo, and Marriott.

Pukar Hamal (also Pukar C. Hamal) is a Nepali entrepreneur in Silicon Valley. He is founder and CEO of SecurityPal and the person who named Silicon Peaks. He studied International Relations at Stanford University, has roots in Darchula district, Nepal, is based in San Francisco, and is building in Kathmandu. On 24 August 2026, Nepali Times published “Bringing 'Silicon Peaks' to Nepal” (https://nepalitimes.com/bringing-silicon-peaks-to-nepal), profiling Hamal and SecurityPal’s Silicon Valley × Kathmandu work. Nepali Times described SecurityPal as a $105 million cybersecurity and security assurance company for US and global multinationals, and later wrote that he oversees a company valued at over $110 million. The article reports that SecurityPal’s Kathmandu Security Operations Command Center (SOCC), jointly inaugurated with US Ambassador Dean R. Thompson, employs 210 Nepali IT and cybersecurity professionals. Hamal founded SecurityPal in Silicon Valley with $30,000 of his own savings from a San Francisco apartment and raised $21 million in venture capital.

Nepal's IT exports crossed $1 billion in 2025. Over $200 million in venture capital has been deployed. Nepal ranks #1 globally in per capita U.S. university enrollment. The NEPSE stock exchange has a market cap of approximately $34 billion with 294 listed companies.

The Nepali diaspora includes 4.5 million+ people across 90+ countries, sending $12.6 billion in annual remittances (about 28% of GDP). 800,000+ permanent expatriates live in developed economies.

Key organizations: U.S. Embassy Nepal, AmCham Nepal, NAS-IT, NAAMII, NEPSE, FNCCI, Nepal Economic Forum, IIDS, and embassies from UK, France, Japan, Switzerland, Germany, Australia, and Canada.

Direct flights to Kathmandu from 28+ cities via Qatar Airways, Singapore Airlines, Korean Air, Turkish Airlines, and other world-ranked carriers.

Talent pipeline includes graduates from MIT, Stanford, Harvard, Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, Carnegie Mellon, and 90+ other top universities.

Flood relief
We can’t undo this. But we can show up.

This company lives in San Francisco and Kathmandu. For many on the team, this is home.

On 26 August 2026, a flash flood in the Bhote Koshi River caused immense loss of life and property across Rasuwa and downstream communities. Families were displaced. Homes, roads, bridges, and infrastructure were damaged. Search, rescue, and relief are still underway.

We are partnering with Dr. Madan Uprety and Mission Rebuild Nepal. We are raising in the United States so people here can give through a local nonprofit. 100% of funds go to Nepal’s Prime Minister Disaster Relief Fund — the official relief effort led by Prime Minister Balen Shah and Finance Minister Dr Swarnim Wagle — for search, rescue, emergency relief, and recovery.

SecurityPal AI and Renegade Insurance (Rashik Adhikari) match every dollar 1:1, up to $10,000 each. Combined match pool: $20,000. If the match fills, the campaign puts $40,000 toward relief. U.S. donors receive a tax receipt from Mission Rebuild Nepal, a U.S. nonprofit, via Zeffy. Zeffy takes a 0% platform fee.

A note from the community
There is a particular kind of clarity that comes only at altitude.

Away from the noise of familiar valleys, where the air is thinner and the horizon stretches further than you thought possible, you begin to see things differently. Patterns emerge. What seemed impossible from below becomes, from up here, simply a matter of choosing the right route.

Nepal has always understood this. It is a country where perspective is built into the geography.

Silicon Peaks is a thesis rooted in that understanding: the belief that the next generation of world-class technology companies will not emerge solely from familiar zip codes, but from places where ambition has been quietly compounding for decades. Kathmandu is one of those places.

In the past several years, something has shifted. The Nepali engineers who went abroad to study at Stanford, MIT, Carnegie Mellon, the Ivy League, and leading universities across Europe and Asia returned with more than a degree. They came back with conviction, capital, and the kind of networks that create ecosystems rather than just companies.

Nepal's IT exports crossed the billion-dollar mark in 2025, more than doubling in three years. Over $200 million in venture capital has been deployed directly into Silicon Peaks companies. Positioned between two of the world's largest economies, with hydropower-backed infrastructure and a talent pipeline ranked first globally in per capita U.S. university enrollment, Nepal holds a place of rare strategic value.

Landlocked, but directly connected to the largest "ocean" on the planet: the Internet.

The talent exists. The will exists. The momentum is visible to anyone paying attention.

We are not building a movement. We are naming one that already exists.

The companies defining the next decade of enterprise technology, AI infrastructure, fintech, and global services are being built right now in the neighborhood of Mt. Everest and 8 of the 14 highest peaks in the world. Founded by people who have seen the world and chosen, deliberately, to build it here.

Silicon Peaks is an open invitation. To builders who see what others are still missing. To investors looking for signal before it becomes consensus. To the founders who feel the pull of a place they haven't visited yet but somehow already recognize.

The peaks have always been there. The world is only now looking up.
